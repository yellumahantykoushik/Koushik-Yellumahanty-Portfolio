Avionics grip mount for the AH-64 Apache cockpit

A telescoping, stowable mount for the pilot mission grips in Boeing's next-generation Apache. The design was sized above MIL-STD pilot loads and checked under three-axis random vibration.

2.14factor of safety (1.5 required)
22.8 ksipeak stress (40 ksi limit)
0.014 inmax vibration deflection
>38 lboverbuilt first iteration, redesigned
Engineering details
  • The mount replaces the TEDAC control system with independently positioned grips. It was an ASU capstone (EGR 402) with a team of 5 and Boeing as sponsor.
  • The first iteration weighed over 38 lb and peaked at only 3.94 MPa, so it was overbuilt. Cut weight by moving non-critical parts to lighter alloys, and replaced the hydraulic gas spring with a simpler gear-locking pivot.
  • Sized for a 300 N pilot input, 30% above the 231 N maximum control force in MIL-STD-1472.
  • Random vibration per ADS-27A-SP in all three axes: 0.014 in longitudinal, 0.007 in lateral, and 0.0047 in vertical deflection.
  • Built a 3D printed PLA scale prototype with a 300 lbf linear actuator and presented it at Boeing design reviews.

Mounting a CubeSat attitude control module

Mount placement and structural analysis for a compact 0.5U reaction wheel ADCS module. I found where launch vibration would fatigue the IMU solder joints and moved the mounts to stiffer regions.

583 Hzfirst natural frequency
27 µm3σ deflection at IMU mounts
20–2000 HzPSD random vibration sweep
54.74°tetrahedral wheel tilt
Engineering details
  • Placed the mounts for a four-wheel tetrahedral reaction wheel layout tilted at 54.74°, which keeps three-axis torque available if one wheel fails.
  • Ran modal analysis feeding into random vibration FEA in Ansys on the 92.5 × 88.9 mm, four-layer FR4 board, using a PSD profile that peaks near 350 Hz. Extracted 10 modes from 583 Hz, the first being global out-of-plane bending.
  • Found 9 µm (1σ) and 27 µm (3σ) deflection at the center-mounted IMU locations, a solder fatigue risk. Relocated the mounts to stiffer frame regions.
  • Modeled theoretical thermal expansion in SolidWorks across PCB burn-in conditions from −40 °C to 125 °C.

Additive Manufacturing · Dec 2025

3D printed micro-supercapacitor electrodes

An ANSYS Maxwell study that separated material effects from geometry effects. It found that electrode shape, not material, drives capacitance.

2.87×planar vs IDE
0%material effect
2.56×electrolyte effect
Details
  • Built matched 12 × 15.4 × 1 mm planar and interdigitated models, run with 20 adaptive passes to 0.5% convergence.
  • PEDOT:PSS, LiCoO₂, graphene, and RuO₂ gave identical capacitance. Planar reached 14.52 pF against 5.06 pF for the IDE.
  • Switching from Na₂SO₄ to air cut capacitance by 1.92× to 2.56×.

NAVSHIP ultrasonic inspection

CIVA simulation compared against 5 MHz immersion data on a steel block with five side-drilled holes, processed through a custom MATLAB pipeline.

−25→−35 dBecho decay

Details
  • Simulated beam profiles at five depths from 38.1 to 63.5 mm to predict how hard deeper flaws would be to detect.
